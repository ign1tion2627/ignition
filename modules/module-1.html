How an Engine Actually Works

What's really happening inside those metal cylinders under the hood — and what "horsepower" actually means.

The Big Idea

A car engine's whole job is simple: turn a tiny explosion into a spinning wheel. That's it. Everything else is just the clever machinery that makes that happen thousands of times a minute.

Most car engines burn a mix of gasoline and air inside metal tubes called cylinders. Each explosion pushes a piston, and that push eventually spins the wheels. A typical car engine has 4, 6, or 8 of these cylinders firing in a careful sequence, over and over, every second you're driving.

Think of it like this

A cylinder is like a tiny cannon that fires hundreds of times a minute — except instead of shooting a cannonball out, it pushes a piston down, and that piston is connected to the wheels.

The Four Strokes

Most engines work in a repeating 4-step cycle. Each step is called a "stroke," because the piston moves up or down once per step.

  1. Intake — the piston moves down, pulling a mix of air and fuel into the cylinder.
  2. Compression — the piston moves back up, squeezing that air-fuel mix into a tiny space.
  3. Combustion — a spark plug ignites the compressed mix. It explodes, slamming the piston back down with force.
  4. Exhaust — the piston moves up one more time, pushing the leftover gas out through the exhaust pipe.

The four-stroke cycle, repeated hundreds of times per minute in every cylinder.

What "Horsepower" Actually Means

You've heard the word a thousand times, but here's what it really is: horsepower is a measurement of how quickly an engine can do work. It comes from over 200 years ago, when engineers needed a way to compare a steam engine's power to the horses it was replacing.

One horsepower is roughly the amount of power it takes to lift 550 pounds one foot in one second. A modern car engine might produce 150–300 horsepower — meaning it can do that same lift 150 to 300 times over, every second.

And torque?

Torque is the twisting force the engine produces — the push that actually gets the wheels turning. Horsepower tells you how fast the engine can work; torque tells you how hard it can push. A truck needs high torque to tow heavy loads; a race car needs high horsepower to reach top speed quickly.
