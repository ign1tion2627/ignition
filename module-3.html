Gas vs. Electric

What's actually different about an EV under the hood — and why it feels so different to drive.

The Big Idea

A gas car and an electric car both do the same basic job — spin the wheels — but they get there in completely different ways. A gas engine burns fuel to create tiny explosions. An electric car uses a motor that spins from electricity alone, with no burning and no explosions at all.

How an Electric Motor Works

Inside an electric motor are magnets and coils of wire. When electricity flows through the coils, it creates a magnetic force that pushes against the magnets — and that push makes the motor spin. No pistons, no cylinders, no combustion. Just electricity in, spinning motion out.

Why EVs feel instant

A gas engine needs time to rev up before it produces full power. An electric motor delivers its full twisting force (torque) the instant power flows to it — which is why EVs can feel like they leap forward the second you press the pedal.

Where the Energy Comes From

A gas car stores energy in a tank of liquid fuel. An EV stores energy in a large battery pack — usually built from hundreds of individual battery cells, similar to (but far bigger than) the batteries in a phone or laptop.

Gas: fuel → burned in engine → wheels. Electric: battery → electric motor → wheels — no burning required.

Regenerative Braking

Here's something a gas car simply can't do: when an EV slows down, its motor can run in reverse, acting like a generator and turning the car's motion back into stored electricity. It's like the wheels "paying back" some of the energy they used, every time you brake. This is called regenerative braking, and it's one of the reasons EVs are more efficient overall.
